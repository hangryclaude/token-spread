Token Spread — where the margin actually is

token-spread · margin model · 2026-08-08

The spread is real. It just isn’t in the accounts.

You don’t make money moving someone else’s subscription around. You make it by serving the same traffic for less than list, on your own key, and keeping the difference. Here is that arithmetic in full, at published rates, with every assumption named.

04

What is assumed, stated plainly

  • 70% cache hitMeasured, and falsified — see below. Real rate is 99.9987%, so this lever is worth nothing on already-cached traffic. Kept here because it still holds for raw, un-optimized API traffic, which is what a customer sends before you touch it.
  • 40% routableOperator-set. There is no per-request difficulty label yet, so this is a what-if, not a claim about which requests are routable.
  • 5% write overheadCache writes assumed at 5% of input volume. Real TTL churn will move this.
  • Rate card 08-08Sonnet intro pricing lapses 2026-08-31. A stale card silently inflates every figure above.
  • A curve, not a pointReport these as a range over the routable fraction, never as one headline number. A single figure invites a challenge you cannot answer.

Slice 1 exists precisely to delete this section — to replace every operator-set assumption with a measured number off real traffic.

05

Then we measured it, and half the model died

Slice 1 ran against 5,026 real events. The 70% cache-hit assumption above was wrong, and not by a little: the measured rate is 99.9987%. Claude Code already caches everything worth caching. The cache lever has no headroom on this traffic — it is worth $0.00.

That kills one of the two levers. It does not kill the business: routing still cuts 20% of spend for every quarter of traffic moved, and 98% of the bill sits on Opus, which is exactly the traffic worth routing.

The door that stays shut

Drawing usage off subscription accounts and transferring it between users is account sharing. It is banned under the terms, and it was repriced to roughly zero margin on 15 Jun 2026 — so it fails on both counts, independently.

It also was never where the money was. Reselling a flat-rate seat is arbitrage on a price gap that has since closed. Everything above runs on your own key, your own invoice, your own optimization — and clears a wider margin than the closed route ever did.
